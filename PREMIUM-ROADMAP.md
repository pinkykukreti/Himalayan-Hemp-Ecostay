## Recommended position

**An intimate ancestral Himalayan retreat, shaped by hemp architecture and village life.**

Keep “A hemp home in the Himalayas” as the supporting line. Lead the guest journey with the home, sleep, food, landscape and hosting; explain hemp as a reason the stay is distinctive. Treat “living laboratory” as a specialist architecture offer, not the first promise to every leisure guest.

Prioritise quiet couples, small groups of friends, and design/culture-minded travellers who welcome a walk and village etiquette. Creative residents can fill selected longer periods if workspaces and connectivity support them. Screen group leaders for compatibility before accepting retreats. High value means suitable guests and healthy contribution per stay, not maximum headcount.

## Days 1–14: make the promise credible

Document beds, bathrooms, room dimensions, privacy, hot water, power, mobile/Wi-Fi reliability, meal options and the true comfortable capacity. Walk and photograph the arrival route; agree a meeting point and realistic luggage support. Confirm a wet-weather plan and access limitations.

Audit mattresses, spotless bathrooms, linen, lighting, insects, drinking water and nighttime comfort. Fix friction before adding decorative luxuries. Agree a host responsible for arrival, meals, housekeeping and problem resolution. Ask recent guests what caused disappointment and what made the stay memorable.

Record the family history with names, dates and old photographs. Obtain consent for family and village stories. Pay local contributors fairly and agree visit boundaries with neighbours. Drinking alcohol, loud music, parties and disruptive noise should be prohibited clearly before booking.

## Days 15–30: pilot a small number of offers

Proposed offer A: a two- or three-night hosted mountain pause. Test a clear package covering the room, specified meals and a gentle introduction to the house, only once delivery is confirmed. Quote arrival/luggage support separately if it cannot be included reliably.

Proposed offer B: whole-house time for a compatible small group, within verified capacity. Price the privacy and host effort; do not squeeze in guests to improve occupancy.

Proposed offer C: a limited architecture immersion or creative residency. Agree learning content, facilitator fees, workspace, connectivity and host availability before advertising dates. Do not launch a broad retreat calendar until actual indoor/weather capacity and facilitation have been tested.

## Pricing method — establish the floor before the premium

Prepare a per-stay cost sheet: food, cleaning/laundry, host labour including owner time, power/water, consumables, arrival support, platform/payment charges and a maintenance allowance. Allocate fixed operating costs across a conservative occupied-night assumption. Calculate break-even and contribution for each offer.

Use this internal planning equation: required gross revenue = (stay costs + fixed-cost allocation + desired contribution) / (1 − channel-fee fraction). Handle taxes according to your accountant's advice. It is a planning method, not a suggested tariff.

Start with a transparent base package, seasonal rules, minimum stay and whole-house supplement. Pilot price changes on comparable dates and compare net contribution, conversion, length of stay and guest-fit feedback. A higher published rate alone does not make the property premium. Exact rates require your costs, occupancy, capacity and service inputs.

## Days 31–60: attract people who want this particular place

Build four content stories: the ancestral reconstruction; sleeping inside the real house; the roadhead-to-house arrival; a day with the hosts and village. Show bathrooms as confidently as mountain views. Retain authentic originals; AI retouching must not invent views, amenities or room size.

Use Instagram and your website to answer real pre-booking questions, with one clear availability path. Keep Airbnb as a source of booking confidence while improving direct enquiry handling. Approach a small number of architecture educators, writers and suitable retreat leaders with a factual capacity/service sheet. Measure qualified enquiries rather than followers. Avoid broad discount campaigns aimed at party groups or drive-in resort expectations.

## Days 61–90: build repeatable hospitality

Use a pre-arrival message covering weather, the walk, packing, food and quiet-house policies; a personal welcome; a simple mid-stay check; and a departure follow-up. Invite every guest to leave an honest review, without incentives or filtering by satisfaction. Track complaints and fix repeated causes. Ask permission before quoting identifiable reviews.

Review monthly: net contribution per stay, occupied nights, average length of stay, enquiry-to-booking conversion, repeat/referral bookings, cancellation causes and recurring guest concerns. Add capacity or retreat dates only if the host team, water/waste systems and village can support them. A top-rated property is an outcome to work toward, never a promise the website should make.

## Owner decisions still needed

When was the house built/rebuilt, and by whom? How many guests can comfortably stay? Who hosts and cooks? What are current rates, inclusions, occupancy and typical costs? What complaints recur? What indoor retreat space and reliable connectivity actually exist? These answers should refine the roadmap before major spending or final pricing.
